Questions owners ask.

Do you only work with accounting and logistics firms?

No. They're the industries I focus on right now, which is why the examples come from them. If your team handles repetitive work through email, documents and spreadsheets, book an audit and I'll tell you whether it's worth automating.

What can you automate, and what can't you?

Repetitive, rule-following work that runs through email, documents, spreadsheets and the software you already use. I won't replace your core business software, and I'll say so in the audit if something isn't worth automating.

How do we know it's working?

Before anything is built, we agree on the tests that define "working" and how we'll measure the result, such as hours saved per week. You get the test report and the numbers.

Do we need anyone technical on our side?

No. I build it, connect it to your systems and set up the alerts. Your team needs to know your process, not the technology, and gets a walkthrough at handover.

How long does a project take?

The audit report arrives within three days. A sprint then builds, tests and launches one workflow in three weeks.

What happens if something breaks after launch?

Alerts tell us before your clients notice. Anything that stops passing the agreed tests is fixed at no charge for 45 days after launch. After that, Ongoing Support covers it.

Will automation replace our staff?

No. It takes the repetitive chasing and retyping off your team, and anything it isn't sure about goes to a person to check.

We're in the US. How does the time difference work?

It helps. I'm in Singapore, so I work on problems you report in your evening during my day, and the fix is often in place by the time you're back.
